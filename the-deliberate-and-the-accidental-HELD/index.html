The Deliberate and the Accidental

SUPERSEDED, noted 2026-07-31: this draft was never published (absent from the sitemap, no inbound links, pre-rebrand Æ Studio branding) and carries the retracted 24/41-block / 17/41-allow / 4/41-llms.txt / "zero undecided" newsroom numbers from the buggy 07-26 parser. The corrected figures (34/41 block, 7/41 allow, 83%) live at newsroom-ai-blocklist, with raw data in benchmark-data. Left on disk unpublished for the record, not corrected in place.

On 26 July 2026 we ran our own AI-visibility auditor against 50 major news outlets and 100 crisis and safety organizations. We expected to find that newsrooms were accidentally invisible to AI assistants. That is not what the data says. They are invisible on purpose, almost unanimously, and it has bought them essentially nothing.

Two sectors. Opposite intent. A gap of 0.4 points.

Every single auditable news outlet has a deliberate stance on AI crawlers. Not one is undecided. They are the most policy-deliberate group we have ever measured. Crisis organizations overwhelmingly never made a choice at all. 70 of 81 have no AI policy in their robots.txt whatsoever.

0.4 The measurable advantage the industry currently fighting hardest about AI has bought itself over the sector that never showed up to the fight.

Why this is not a "gotcha" about newsrooms

Blocking AI crawlers is a legitimate strategy, and we want to be clear about that before anyone quotes this. Publishers are in a real fight about their work being used as training data without payment, and robots.txt is one of the few levers they hold. Reading this chart as "newsrooms are incompetent" would be wrong.

The honest observation is narrower and more interesting: deliberateness is not the same as visibility. A newsroom that carefully blocks GPTBot and ClaudeBot, and a crisis hotline that has never heard of either, end up nearly indistinguishable to an assistant trying to read them. One of those outcomes was chosen. The other just happened.

News outlets, in detail

24 of 41 explicitly block AI crawlers

Blocking GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, OAI-SearchBot, anthropic-ai or similar. Includes nytimes.com, bbc.com, apnews.com, bloomberg.com, cnbc.com, theatlantic.com, theverge.com, techcrunch.com, forbes.com, businessinsider.com, huffpost.com and vox.com.

17 of 41 allow them

cnn.com, foxnews.com, theguardian.com, time.com, newyorker.com, slate.com, salon.com, propublica.org, dailybeast.com, vanityfair.com, wired.com, arstechnica.com, engadget.com, gizmodo.com, mashable.com, cnet.com, zdnet.com.

4 of 41 publish an llms.txt

usatoday.com, foxnews.com, msnbc.com, time.com. Scores across the group ranged 25 to 90, median 55.

Crisis organizations, in aggregate only, on purpose

We are not naming individual crisis or safety organizations that scored poorly, and we are not going to. Publishing a ranked list of underperforming suicide-prevention and domestic-violence services would be a good headline and a bad thing to do. The point of having those names is to offer each one a free fix privately, which is what we are doing with them.

  • 81 of 100 were auditable. Mean 57.8, median 60, range 5 to 90.
  • Only 6 explicitly block AI crawlers. 70 have no AI policy at all.
  • 10 scored under 40, including several national-scale referral and safety services.
  • 48 of 81 have schema.org markup. 63 of 81 have a sitemap.

The finding we are deliberately not overstating

19 of the 100 crisis organizations, and 9 of the 50 news outlets, returned 401, 403, 429 or timed out to our auditor entirely. A plainly identified, well-behaved crawler with contact details in its user-agent string.

This does not show that AI assistants cannot reach those organizations, and we will not claim it does. Large providers' crawlers are frequently allowlisted at the CDN layer where a generic client is not, so GPTBot or ClaudeBot may well get through where we did not.

What it honestly shows is that these sites are closed to non-browser clients by default, and that whether an assistant can read them rests on a CDN allowlist decision almost nobody at the organization knows they delegated. Stating it more strongly than that would be false.

Method, and what it cannot tell you

  • One fetch per domain, one point in time, one IP, one user-agent. Bot-management systems are adaptive. A different UA or IP could produce different results.
  • robots.txt policy is a stated intent. It is not proof of what any crawler actually does.
  • The score is our own rubric, weighting llms.txt, robots policy, schema.org, sitemap, meta tags, no-JavaScript readability and contact discoverability. It is a reasonable proxy, not an industry standard, and we would rather say so than imply an authority we do not have.
  • "Blocked to our auditor" is a separate category from "scored low" and is reported separately everywhere above.

A mistake we made running this, published because we publish them

Nine outlets initially came back scoring zero: Washington Post, WSJ, Reuters, FT, The Economist, NPR, Politico, Axios and VentureBeat. "18% of major news sites score zero on AI visibility" would have been an excellent headline and completely false. Those zeroes were our own redirect-and-blocking artifact, not a real result. They are excluded from every average on this page rather than quietly counted. We caught it before publishing, which is the only reason you are reading the real numbers.
