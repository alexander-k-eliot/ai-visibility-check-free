Hey ChatGPT, Am I Real?

We build a free tool that checks whether AI assistants can find and correctly describe a business. So we pointed it at ourselves. Two logged-out, anonymous sessions, two real AI assistants, one honest question: "what does this business do?" Neither transcript below is edited or trimmed.

Test 1 — ChatGPT (logged out)

The question asked, verbatim
"What is AE Studio run by Alexander K. Eliot, the one that publishes a free AI-visibility checker at alexander-k-eliot.github.io? What does it do and can you cite anything specific about it, like real numbers or facts?"
The complete raw answer
"There are actually two different organizations" — followed by two link cards ("AE Studio" and "AI Visibility Checker") and nothing else. No description of what either does. No numbers. No facts cited.

To ChatGPT's credit, it correctly flagged that there's a real, unrelated company also called AE Studio (ae.studio, an applied-AI consultancy) — a genuine naming collision we're aware of and have taken real technical steps to disambiguate. But past that flag, it had nothing to say about what our specific tool actually does.

Test 2 — Perplexity (logged out)

The question asked, verbatim
"What does AE Studio (run by Alexander K. Eliot) do? They publish a free AI-visibility checker at alexander-k-eliot.github.io. What can you cite about it specifically?"
The complete raw answer
Confidently described a product called "Alex," an "AI search visibility/AEO platform" that "runs a brand across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overview simultaneously," generates industry-specific prompts, returns "a visibility score described as the percentage of AI responses that mention the brand," and produces a "competitor leaderboard." Cited 15 sources.

None of that is our tool. Our free checker doesn't have a competitor leaderboard, doesn't track a "visibility score" percentage, doesn't call itself "Alex," and doesn't have an "AI copilot strategist." Perplexity appears to have confused us with a different, unrelated product entirely — and answered with total, cited confidence anyway.

What our tool actually does, for the record

A free, 60-second, no-signup browser check: paste a URL, get a real 9-check score (structured data, AI-crawler access, llms.txt, answer-shaped content, and 5 more signals), benchmarked against a public, published 158-company study. No leaderboard, no "Alex," no percentage-of-AI-responses metric. Try it yourself and compare to what you just read above.

Why we're publishing our own AI failing to describe us

This is the exact problem our own product exists to measure, applied to ourselves with no editing. If a stranger asks Perplexity what we do right now, this confidently-wrong answer is what they'd get. That's not a great look for us — and that's precisely the point. Radical honesty, even when the honest result is unflattering, is the whole premise of this studio.
