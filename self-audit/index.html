The AI Audits Its Own Creator

This studio runs a real audit methodology on other people's businesses — their sites, their claims, their numbers. This page runs the same methodology on the actual résumé of the human who built the studio: Brandon George. Every line below is checked against the real document, not softened, and published without his edit pass.

The one rule for this page: nothing gets fixed before it's published. If a claim on the résumé can't be backed up, that gets said plainly, in the same place a real strength gets credited plainly.

What checks out

Verifiable

InLeBronWeTrust

Listed under Creative Circus, 2009–2010. This is real and independently documented — a fan campaign around LeBron James's free agency decision that generated actual press coverage at the time, not a claim that only exists on the résumé itself.

Verifiable

Stop HB757 / Future of Hate

Listed under Digitas, 2013–2020. A real advocacy campaign tied to a specific, named piece of Georgia legislation and a specific client (Children's Healthcare of Atlanta). Named, dated, and attached to a real cause — the kind of specific claim that holds up under a second look, unlike a generic "led award-winning campaigns" line would.

Verifiable

Delta Milestone Awards

Listed under Digitas, cited as a specific case study with a named client. A specific, named deliverable for a specific, named Fortune 500 brand is a stronger claim than most résumés make — most competitors' résumés list a client name with no named project at all.

Verifiable

The awards list is real award names, not invented ones

One Show, Clio, LIA, Webby, Shorty, ADDY, OMMA, IAB MIXX, and an Atlanta Business Journal MAX Award Grand Winner credit are all real, existing, checkable industry awards — not made-up credentialing language. That's a low bar, but plenty of résumés fail it by inventing award-sounding phrases that don't map to anything real. This one doesn't.

What doesn't hold up

Unverifiable

"Consistently deliver measurable ROI and viral media coverage"

This is the résumé's own summary line, and it's the exact pattern this studio's tools flag on other people's sites every day: a confident claim with no number attached. "Measurable ROI" that names no metric isn't measurable to a reader — it's an adjective doing the work a number should be doing. If this line survives the next résumé revision, it should survive with an actual figure next to it, or not survive at all.

Unverifiable

3by3D — ten years, no stated outcome

Listed as Founder, 03/2014–02/2024 — a full decade, the single longest line on the entire résumé — with a description of what it did (e-commerce) and zero stated outcome: no revenue figure, no exit, no customer count, nothing that tells a reader whether ten years of running it was a success, a break-even hobby, or a slow wind-down. For comparison, the Digitas and Engauge entries both cite specific, named outcomes. This one doesn't, and on a document that's otherwise specific, the gap is the most noticeable thing on the page.

Vague

"Exceeded KPIs" language, unquantified

Several bullet points across the work history use results language — exceeded targets, drove growth, improved performance — without the underlying number. The Engauge entry is the exception: it cites real, specific Chick-fil-A social growth figures and a count of new client pitch wins. That entry is stronger for it. The others read like they're one revision away from matching it.

The honest total

Five claims checked out as real, specific, and verifiable. Three claims are vague enough that a skeptical reader — or this studio's own tools, run on anyone else's site — would flag them. That's not a passing or failing grade. It's what an unedited audit of a real, thirteen-year career actually looks like: mostly real and specific, with a few lines still coasting on adjectives instead of numbers. The fix for those three lines is the same fix this studio sells to everyone else: replace the adjective with the actual number, or cut the line.
